# Robustness check: bootstrap intervals and guardrail power

Generated by `python3 scripts/robustness.py` after the main pipeline.

## Do the intervals hold up without the normal approximation?

I resampled users within each arm 2,000 times (seed 2718) and took the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the difference.

| Metric | Wald 95% CI | Bootstrap 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| 7-day activation lift | +1.90 pp to +5.15 pp | +1.85 pp to +5.15 pp |
| 7-day support-contact change | +1.49 pp to +3.03 pp | +1.51 pp to +3.01 pp |

The two methods agree to within a few hundredths of a point. With roughly 6,000 users per arm and success counts in the hundreds, that is what I expected, and the HOLD decision does not depend on the interval method: even the lower end of the support increase is above the +0.50 pp limit.

## Could this experiment have passed the guardrail at all?

The guardrail passes only if the upper bound of the support increase is at most +0.50 pp. With a 3.76% baseline and about 6,000 users per arm:

- A treatment with **no effect at all** on support would pass only **30%** of the time (normal approximation).
- Simulating 1,000 harmless experiments and applying the same rule gives **30%**, so the formula is not the problem.
- 80% power at this margin needs about **22,730 users per arm**, almost four times the sample.
- At the current size, the tightest margin a harmless treatment clears with 80% power is about **+0.97 pp**.

## What I take from it

The hold is still the right call here, because the observed harm is large. The design, though, would also have blocked most genuinely harmless onboarding changes. For the next test I would agree one of these before collecting data: a sample of roughly 22,730 users per arm, or a margin of about +1.0 pp that the support team has signed off. Picking either after seeing results would undo the point of a pre-registered rule.
